Spin misalignment, initial conditions, and model assumptions

What “misaligned spins” means here

The initial canonical orbital angular momentum is J₀ = r₀ × p₀. The initial x–y plane is perpendicular to J₀, and +z points along J₀. The spin tilt θₐ is measured from +z; the azimuth φₐ is measured from +x toward +y in the x–y plane. The index a labels body 1 or 2.

Misalignment criterion: cos θₐ = (Sₐ · J₀) / (|Sₐ| |J₀|). A nonzero spin is misaligned with the orbital axis when 0° < θₐ < 180°, equivalently Sₐ × J₀ ≠ 0. The special values θₐ = 0° and 180° mean aligned and anti-aligned, respectively.

Orbit and cycle convention

This is an eccentric, conservative binary, with no radiation reaction. Units are G = c = M = 1. The Newtonian reference semimajor axis is a = 40 and eccentricity is e = 0.35. The initial relative position is r₀ = (26, 0, 0), and canonical momentum is p₀ = (0, 0.24√(1.35/26), 0). These are shared initial canonical data, not equal initial velocities or energies across the models. The reference e is not an identical relativistic eccentricity for all cases.

One radial cycle runs from a minimum of separation (periastron) to the next. The spatial orbit, spins, and orbital-plane orientation need not return to their initial values. Generic spin–orbit motion departs from the initial plane within the first cycle. Ten cycles make accumulated precession easier to see; they are not required for nonplanarity.

Interactions in each case

  1. Newtonian: Newtonian orbital Hamiltonian only.
  2. Orbital 1PN: Newtonian plus 1PN orbital terms.
  3. Orbital 2PN: Newtonian, 1PN, and 2PN orbital terms.
  4. Spin–orbit 1.5PN: Newtonian and 1PN orbital terms plus leading spin–orbit coupling.
  5. Orbital 2PN plus spin–orbit: case 4 with the 2PN orbital term added.
  6. Mutual spin–spin: case 5 with the leading interaction between S₁ and S₂. Self-spin terms are deliberately omitted in this diagnostic case.
  7. Full conservative 2PN: case 6 plus both black-hole spin-induced quadrupole terms.

Cases 1–3: all spin couplings are switched off. The spin arrows are passive, fixed reference vectors; their misalignment does not make these orbital-only trajectories nonplanar. Cases 4–7: the spins evolve with the selected interactions.

How to read the picture

The shaded plane can have normal r × p (canonical plane) or r × ṙ (position–velocity plane). These generally differ with spin–orbit coupling. The reported orbital tilt refers to r × p. Body locations use the leading centre-of-mass split x₁ = 0.4r and x₂ = −0.6r, without higher-PN centre-of-mass corrections. Body sizes are enlarged. Each arrow has its own fixed display scale; compare its changes over time, not its length with another arrow. Precession angles and departures from the initial plane are not exaggerated.

The live energy, total-angular-momentum, and spin-length diagnostics check the displayed state. J orbital² and J orbital · S effective are conserved in cases 1–5; their variation can be physical in cases 6–7. The simulation evolves the selected truncated Hamiltonian numerically; it is not a full general-relativistic solution.
